# Creative direction: make every motion serve the story

## Non-negotiable brief
A director's brief contains goal, one-sentence audience tension, narrator's exact text or speechless intent, duration, audio ownership, target platform, aspect, format, dominant visual metaphor, one chosen style, and one central payoff. Source claims require verifiable evidence; never invent usage counts, revenue figures, rankings, screenshots or creator quotes.

## Primary compositional pass
Describe scene by scene with **physical nouns** and **behaviors**. Examples: an invoice paper stack flips into a workflow diagram; a browser result travels along a connector to a resolved checklist; a hand-drawn circle becomes the chapter pointer. Prioritize a silhouette readable without subtitles. Use scale hierarchy (hero 1, proof 2, accent 3), color meanings, and negative space. A visual cue should precede or coincide with a spoken keyword, not arrive a second late.

## Motion style and texture pass
Specify: background material, surface roughness/shadows, primary font and fallback, pixel margins, tracking, geometric radius, line-weight, element count, duration per animation, type of easing, parallax layer offset, visual motion anchors, and color semantics. Name exactly which items carry between shots. No generic full-screen gradient/fade every second. Make static screenshots meaningful via pointer movement, crop, highlights or actual UI transitions.

## Director's shot question
For every scene: what changes visually? What does the viewer learn? Where does attention land? What stays on screen long enough to read? Why is this movement justified? If the answer is merely 'motion makes it exciting,' redesign it.

## Five common quality failures
- Full-frame text dumped onto an empty gradient.
- An arbitrary unrelated image/robot next to every caption.
- Bouncy overshoot everywhere instead of soft-settle.
- Flashing data before the viewer can identify context/source.
- Closing with a CTA while the actual promised payoff remains unresolved.

## Honest adaptation
OpenNolan's source describes creator-oriented hooks, programmatic editorial motion, source-backed evidence, and animation workflows. This new project abstracts those ideas into a renderer-independent brief; it does not depend on OpenNolan-specific internal tools, files, or Notion databases.
